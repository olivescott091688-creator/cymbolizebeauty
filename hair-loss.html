Specialties

Types of Hair Loss Cymonne Specializes In

Every type of hair loss is different — and each one requires a different approach. Here's what Cymonne works with every day.

💊

GLP-1 Related Hair Loss

Medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, and other GLP-1 agonists have been linked to significant hair thinning and loss in many patients. This type is growing rapidly as these medications become more widespread.

Mesh Integration and Invisible Bead Wefts are both excellent options — Cymonne will assess which suits your current hair density.

🎗️

Chemotherapy & Treatment Hair Loss

Hair loss during or after cancer treatment is one of the most emotionally significant experiences a woman can go through. Cymonne approaches these clients with deep care, flexible timing, and solutions designed for scalp sensitivity.

Timing matters — reach out and Cymonne will help you understand the best approach for your stage of treatment or recovery.

🌿

Alopecia

Alopecia areata, androgenetic alopecia, traction alopecia — each presents differently and requires a tailored plan. Cymonne has experience with all forms and will work around your specific pattern of loss.

Mesh Integration was specifically designed with alopecia clients in mind — it doesn't require a continuous hair base to attach to.

🌊

Hormonal Thinning

Postpartum hair loss, perimenopause, thyroid-related thinning, and stress-induced shedding all fall under this category. The hair can become significantly finer and more fragile — traditional extensions often make things worse.

Cymonne specializes in methods that add volume and length without putting stress on fragile strands.

✨

Fine & Fragile Hair

Some women are simply born with fine, fragile hair that has never been able to hold traditional extensions. This doesn't mean extensions are off the table — it means you need the right technique and the right stylist.

Invisible Bead Wefts and Mesh Integration are both specifically suited to fine, fragile hair. Neither requires heat or glue at the attachment point.

The Problem

Why Traditional Extensions Don't Work for Hair Loss

Most extension methods — keratin bonds, tape-ins, clip-ins, standard sew-ins — are designed for clients who already have a healthy head of full hair to work with. They rely on:

  • ✕ A dense base of hair to anchor into or sew against
  • ✕ Hair strong enough to withstand the weight of wefts or bonds
  • ✕ Enough coverage to hide attachment points from underneath
  • ✕ Strands resilient enough to be sectioned, pulled, and braided tightly

For women experiencing hair loss, none of those things may be true. The result? Extensions that don't lie flat, attachment points that show through, and — worse — methods that put so much stress on fragile strands that they accelerate the thinning.

A well-meaning stylist who doesn't know how to adapt can actually cause more damage than help.

The Result for Many Women

  • → Turned away by stylists who "can't work with that"
  • → Given extensions that look unnatural or fall out quickly
  • → Extensions that cause further traction hair loss
  • → Told to "wait until your hair grows back" — with no guidance on how
  • → Giving up entirely on finding a solution

This is exactly the problem Cymonne built her practice to solve.

The Solution

The Mesh Integration Difference

Mesh Integration uses a fine, breathable mesh base that sits gently against the scalp. Hair — either human hair wefts or individual strands — is woven through the mesh, creating a natural-looking system that:

  • ✦ Works on virtually any hair density — even very sparse areas
  • ✦ Does not require a dense base to attach to
  • ✦ Is completely breathable — scalp health is maintained
  • ✦ Moves and behaves like natural hair
  • ✦ Can be customized to match your exact hair color and texture
  • ✦ Can be styled, washed, and maintained like natural hair
  • ✦ Does not put traction or stress on fragile existing hair
